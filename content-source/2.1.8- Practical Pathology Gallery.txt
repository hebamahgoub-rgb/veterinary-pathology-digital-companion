    <section class="self-assessment">
      <h2>Self-Assessment</h2>
      
      <div class="question-block">
        <p class="question">1. Which morphological feature best separates hepatic lipid steatosis (Case 2) from hepatic glycogen accumulation (Case 3)?</p>
        <details>
          <summary>Show Answer</summary>
          <p>In lipid steatosis, sharply defined vacuoles may displace the nucleus toward the cell periphery. Glycogen more often produces diffuse or feathery cytoplasmic clearing with relatively central nuclei.</p>
        </details>
      </div>

      <div class="question-block">
        <p class="question">2. Which special stain can distinguish the golden-brown pigment in Case 7 from the black exogenous pigment in Case 8?</p>
        <details>
          <summary>Show Answer</summary>
          <p>Perls’ Prussian blue stains hemosiderin blue because it contains iron. Carbon does not produce this reaction.</p>
        </details>
      </div>

      <div class="question-block">
        <p class="question">3. What optical equipment is required to demonstrate the characteristic birefringence of Congo-red-stained amyloid in Case 9?</p>
        <details>
          <summary>Show Answer</summary>
          <p>A microscope equipped with crossed polarizing filters.</p>
        </details>
      </div>
