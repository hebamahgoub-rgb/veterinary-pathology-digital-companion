Scope & Editorial Purpose

Part of the Veterinary Pathology Digital Companion

VetPath Magazine is the editorial publication of the Veterinary Pathology Digital Companion. While the Digital Companion focuses on connecting pathological mechanisms with gross and microscopic findings for undergraduate and postgraduate learners, VetPath Magazine examines how veterinary science and animal-health events intersect with public health, food markets, legislation, and the veterinary profession.

Coverage is organized into five permanent subject sections: Research & Pathology, Public Health & One Health, Economics & Trade, Policy & Public Affairs, and Veterinary Practice & Education. Each article lives on one permanent page beneath its primary section and may also be linked from related sections or themed collections as the archive grows.

Editorship & Independence

Academic & Non-Commercial

VetPath Magazine is founded and edited by Prof. Hebatallah Ahmed Mahgoub, Professor of Pathology at the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, Mansoura University, Egypt.

  • Educational & scholarly mandate: Articles are written for veterinary students, pathologists, clinicians, researchers, and informed readers following animal health and food-system policy in Egypt and internationally.
  • Editorial independence: The publication is non-commercial and carries no paid placements, sponsored content, or industry-funded endorsements.
  • Companion integration: Where relevant, magazine articles cross-link to anatomical, diagnostic, and postgraduate reference pages within the Veterinary Pathology Digital Companion.

Sourcing & Verification

Three-Tier Evidence Standard
Source Tier Examples Editorial Role
Tier 1
Primary Scientific, Surveillance & Legal Authorities 		Peer-reviewed journals; official outbreak databases (WOAH WAHIS); national and international agencies (AHRI, NFSA, MALR/GOVS, CAPMAS, FAO, WHO, EFSA); legal and trade registries (FAOLEX, WTO SPS). Primary spine for pathological mechanisms, case counts, statistical baselines, and statutory rules.
Tier 2
Policy Institutes & Analytical Reports 		Independent food-policy, livestock, and economic research centers (IFPRI, ILRI, ECES, Chatham House, Carnegie Middle East Center) and USDA FAS country reports. Economic modeling, market-structure context, food-security assessments, and cross-country policy comparisons.
Tier 3
Regional & International Reporting 		Established Egyptian, regional, and international newspapers and specialist science or veterinary newsrooms (such as EnterpriseAM Egypt, Daily News Egypt, Al-Mal News, Ahram Online, Reuters, CIDRAP, Vet Record). Tracking breaking events, market prices, ministerial statements, and public debate—corroborated against Tier 1 or Tier 2 sources for scientific or legal claims.

Source Languages & Scientific Figures

Transparency in Translation & Visuals

English publication with original-language links: VetPath Magazine publishes in English. Whenever an article draws on an Arabic, French, or other non-English decree, statistical bulletin, or news report, we link directly to the original source and identify its language in the source note. Quotations translated into English are checked against the original text for technical accuracy.

Source-note convention: Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation, Veterinary Quarantine Update, Nov 2026 [Arabic]; CAPMAS Producer Price Index Bulletin [Arabic/English].

Figures and visual attribution: Original photography, open-access or cited peer-reviewed figures, and educational diagrams are clearly distinguished in captions. Any synthetic or schematic educational reconstruction used to illustrate a pathological mechanism is explicitly labeled as such, without invented case histories or unverified magnification bars.

Corrections & Updates

Accountability Record

Substantive corrections: If an article contains a factual, statistical, taxonomic, or translation error, we correct the text promptly and append a dated Correction note at the bottom of the article stating what was changed and when.

Developing-story updates: When an outbreak report, trade restriction, or legislative proposal evolves after publication, we add a dated Update note so readers can distinguish new developments from the original reporting.

Flagging an error: Readers who spot an inaccuracy, broken primary link, or translation nuance are encouraged to report it to the editor via the Digital Companion's contact channel.
